<!-- STAGE

Digital technologies rely on mineral extraction as their primary material input. Coltan and other rare earth elements are sourced through intensive labor and ecological disruption, forming a physical foundation of the technosphere.

---

<!-- STAGE
id: assembly
roman: II
title: Assembly
subtitle: Material Transformation
position: above
image: /src/assets/assembly.jpg
alt: "Workers in protective suits on an electronics assembly line in a clean room."
-->

Extracted materials are transformed into digital devices through industrial manufacturing processes. These environments convert raw minerals into computational hardware, embedding human labor into objects that are later marketed as immaterial or magical.

---

<!-- STAGE
id: computation
roman: III
title: Computation
subtitle: Energetic Processing
position: below
image: /src/assets/computation.jpg

-->

Data centers function as metabolic 'organs' of the digital system. They store information, process user behavior, and consume vast amounts of (often non-renewable) energy to sustain continuous operation, linking digital activity, known to users as the cloud, directly to environmental cost.

---

<!-- STAGE
id: circulation
roman: IV
title: Circulation
subtitle: Systemic Transmission
position: above
image: /src/assets/circulation.png

-->

Global connectivity is made possible through physical infrastructures that circulate data across continents. Undersea cables operate as the circulatory and nervous systems of the internet, enabling real-time 'wireless' exchange while remaining materially fragile.

---

<!-- STAGE

roman: V
title: Waste
subtitle: Disposal and Externalization

The technosphere produces waste as an inevitable outcome of consumption. Discarded electronics, toxic chemicals, and environmental degradation accumulates in regions distant from use, externalizing environmental and health impacts from the marvel of our digital technologies.
