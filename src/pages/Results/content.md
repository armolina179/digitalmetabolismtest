Result: You Are Here
This moment marks a point of orientation. To be “here” is not to stand outside digital systems, nor to be enclosed within them, but to exist alongside overlapping flows of material, energy, labor, and culture. Your digital life unfolds in relation to these systems, moving with them, shaping them, and being shaped in return.

A. Origins
Materials drawn from the earth (metals, minerals, and chemical compounds) are extracted, refined, and transported through global supply chains. These origins remain present, even as devices feel seamless or immaterial. To use digital technology is to remain connected to these beginnings through energy, infrastructure, and labor.

B. Data
Information circulates through physical systems; servers, cables, data centers, and transfer stations, sustained by energy and constant maintenance. Every interaction leaves a trace within this flow, situating your digital actions within a continuous exchange rather than a distant abstraction.

C. Afterlives
Devices do not disappear when they are replaced or discarded. They continue on as electronic waste, often traveling elsewhere to be dismantled and recycled, or more often than not, left to accumulate. These afterlives link moments of use to extended material timelines that affect environments and human bodies.

D. Media
Images, platforms, and streams shape how attention moves and how culture forms. Media does not sit on top of technology. It moves through it, influencing both social relationships and the infrastructures that sustain digital life. Participation in media is participation in this ongoing circulation.

Reflecting on Digital Metabolism
Inspired by principles of permaculture, digital metabolism can be understood through relationships of care—care for the earth, care for people, and care for shared resources. These ethics do not offer instructions, but points of attention: ways of noticing how digital practices intersect with ecological and human systems.

Earth Care: Attending to the material and energetic costs of digital systems.
People Care: Recognizing the labor and bodies that sustain technological infrastructures.
Fair Share: Considering balance, restraint, and reciprocity in digital culture.

“You are here” is not a conclusion. It is a reminder that digital life is lived in the present tense—co-existent with the systems that sustain it, and open to being reimagined.
